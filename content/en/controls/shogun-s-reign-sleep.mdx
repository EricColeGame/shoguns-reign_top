## The Core Loop: Ryō, Reputation, Rank

The advertised loop is refreshingly legible. You arrive with almost nothing, earn Ryō through farming, labour and trades, and convert that money and effort into better standing. Reputation and rank are the long-term currencies; Ryō is the short-term one.

## Choosing What to Do First

Because rank requirements are undocumented, the smart early approach is breadth, not optimisation. Trying several forms of work teaches you which ones you can perform reliably, how the environment reacts, and where other players congregate. It also prevents you from committing to a grind that was quietly rebalanced.

Player practice, not documented mechanics: many players treat the first stretch as a tour rather than a job. Walk the roads, look at the towns and shrines, and note which areas feel busy. Busy areas tend to be where trades happen and where reputation-building interactions occur, but that is a community pattern, not a rule the developer has published.

Once you have a sense of the map, narrowing toward a profession makes sense. The official description promises professions can be mastered, so specialising is a supported path. It just does not tell you the list, the cost, or the payoff.

## Roleplay Etiquette in an Edo Setting

Shogun's Reign is described as a roleplaying and progression experience, which means the social layer matters as much as the numbers. Etiquette here is player convention, not documented mechanics, but it shapes how quickly you get included.

A few widely used habits:

- Bow when entering someone's space, and wait to be addressed by higher-ranked players before speaking at length.
- Keep out-of-character chatter clearly separated from in-character speech so scenes are not disrupted.
- Ask before touching another player's property, stall or workspace.
- Do not assume a title gives you authority over a scene; ask who is running it.
- Be patient with newcomers, since the game is new and the population is large.

None of this is enforced by a rulebook you can read. It is simply how a lot of players behave, and matching it tends to reduce friction.

## Climbing Toward Shogun

The headline ambition is explicit: rank up, accumulate reputation, and compete for a single Shogun position. That scarcity is the design. There is one seat, which means the top of the ladder is inherently contested rather than cooperative.

What is not documented is how the position changes hands, whether it is decided by rank, reputation, a vote, an event, or something else entirely. Third-party guides occasionally claim to know, but the official listing does not describe it. If the Shogun race is your goal, the honest advice is to build wealth and reputation steadily while watching official update notes, because the rules around authority can shift in a game this young.
