Every campaign in Shogun's Reign eventually comes down to one question: who is still standing when the dust settles? Armies win battles, but they rarely hold a domain together on their own. The Shogun's Reign physician is the role that keeps everything else alive between those battles — patching up wounded retainers, smothering outbreaks before they empty a village, and quietly deciding which characters live long enough to matter.

That is exactly why veteran players treat the Shogun's Reign physician as the engine of long-term stability rather than a background NPC. Ignore the role, and hard-won momentum drains away one fever at a time. Invest in it, and your domain keeps producing soldiers, taxes, and loyalty while your rivals are still burying theirs.

## What the Shogun's Reign Physician Actually Does

The physician is a support and utility role, not a frontline one. Your value shows up in the numbers behind the numbers: how many retainers recover, how fast a settlement returns to full output, and how many crises never happen at all.

- **Emergency treatment.** Stabilizing wounded characters after battles, accidents, or assassination attempts. Fast, correct treatment prevents permanent debuffs that can cripple a key retainer for the rest of a campaign.
- **Remedy crafting and supply.** Turning gathered herbs, cloth, and other materials into usable medicines. Physicians who control their own supply chain rarely get caught short during a crisis.
- **Outbreak control.** Identifying a spreading illness early, isolating affected characters, and slowing transmission before it reaches your lord's household.
- **Household upkeep.** Long-term care for aging or chronically unwell characters. In many campaigns this is invisible work that quietly protects your most valuable relationships.
- **Access and information.** A trusted healer moves through private quarters and hears private conversations. Player experience suggests that this access is one of the role's most underrated advantages.

Reputation compounds here more than in almost any other role. Keep a lord's family alive through a bad season and doors open that coin alone cannot buy.

## Core Mechanics: Diagnosis, Treatment, and Prevention

### Diagnosis comes before treatment

Most treatment systems follow a diagnosis-first loop. Acting on a guess wastes limited materials, and in some cases makes the condition worse. Physicians with stronger observation or medical knowledge see clearer symptom descriptions, which means fewer misdiagnoses and less wasted stock. If you are unsure what you are looking at, observe, ask, and wait rather than treating blind.

### Treatment is a resource problem

Every treatment spends something: bandages, herbs, medicine, coin, or simply time you could have spent elsewhere. That turns the physician into a triage manager. Do you spend your last rare remedy on a high-value retainer, or hold it in case an outbreak hits the main household tomorrow? There is no universally correct answer, but there is always a cost.

### Prevention pays better than cure

Community reports consistently point to the same conclusion: physicians who invest in prevention outperform those who only react. Clean water, sanitation, adequate rest rotations, and early quarantine all reduce the number of emergencies you face. Prevention is unglamorous, but it is the difference between a calm domain and a permanent crisis queue.

The table below reflects how players typically categorize problems. Exact names and effects vary by build.

| Ailment category | Common warning signs | Typical physician response |
|---|---|---|
| Wounds and injuries | Visible damage, reduced performance | Immediate stabilization, then recovery care |
| Fevers and infections | Persistent weakness, declining output | Isolation, supportive treatment, monitoring |
| Poisoning | Sudden onset, unusual symptoms | Identify the source, then treat and investigate |
| Fatigue and stress | Slow decline, refusal to work | Rest scheduling and workload reduction |
| Outbreaks | Multiple cases in one location | Quarantine, sanitation, supply stockpiling |

## Skill Priorities and Build Paths

New physicians tend to spread points everywhere and end up mediocre at all of it. A better approach is to sequence your investment: secure the basics, then build depth.

| Phase | Priority focus | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Basic treatment and gathering | You cannot heal what you cannot supply |
| Early–mid | Diagnosis accuracy | Fewer wasted materials and fewer bad outcomes |
| Mid | Crafting efficiency | Stretches scarce ingredients across more patients |
| Mid–late | Prevention and administration | Reduces how many emergencies reach you at all |
| Late | Specialization | Deep expertise unlocks outcomes generalists cannot reach |

Once the fundamentals are in place, most players settle into one of three broad playstyles. These are archetypes rather than fixed classes, and community reports describe them slightly differently from server to server, but the core trade-offs hold up.

| Playstyle | Focus | Strengths | Weaknesses | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Field Medic | Battlefield and rapid response | Saves retainers others would lose | Consumes supplies fast | Aggressive, war-heavy campaigns |
| Court Apothecary | Crafting, research, household care | Reliable supply, strong relationships | Slower in a crisis | Long, economy-focused runs |
| Plague Warden | Prevention and public health | Prevents large-scale disasters | Low immediate payoff | Dense settlements, high-population domains |

The Shogun's Reign physician who tries to do all three at once usually does none of them well. Pick a lane, then borrow selectively from the others once your core is solid.

## Getting Started: A Practical Sequence

If you are picking up the role for the first time, follow a deliberate order instead of reacting to whatever happens next.

| Step | Action | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Secure a steady supply of basic materials | Treatment stops the moment your stock runs dry |
| 2 | Learn your settlement's routine and weak points | Most problems start in predictable places |
| 3 | Build a small buffer before the first crisis | A reserve buys you time when it matters most |
| 4 | Establish relationships with key households | Trust unlocks access and better information |
| 5 | Add prevention measures to your weekly routine | Fewer emergencies means more room to specialize |

A few habits separate effective physicians from busy ones:

- Log what you treat and what you spend. Patterns show up fast.
- Treat the cause, not just the symptom — a recurring fever usually means a recurring source.
- Keep one reserve you never touch unless a lord's household is involved.
- Communicate. A physician who explains risk clearly gets cooperation instead of resistance.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | What it looks like | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Treating before diagnosing | Burning supplies on the wrong condition | Observe first, then act |
| Hoarding everything | Refusing to spend until it is too late | Set a clear threshold for when to spend |
| Ignoring prevention | Constant firefighting | Schedule sanitation and rest upkeep |
| Spreading skills thin | Mediocre at everything | Commit to one playstyle first |
| Neglecting relationships | No access when you need it | Invest in trust early and often |
| Skipping records | Repeating the same failures | Track treatments, costs, and outcomes |

The most common complaint from community reports is not a lack of skill points — it is a lack of planning. Physicians who decide in advance what they will and will not spend rarely get caught off guard.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Shogun's Reign physician worth playing if I prefer action?**

Yes, if you enjoy influence over direct combat. The role wins through attrition and stability rather than duels. Player experience suggests physicians often shape a campaign's outcome more than any single fighter, simply because they decide who survives long enough to keep fighting.

**What should a new Shogun's Reign physician focus on first?**

Basic treatment and a reliable material supply. Everything else — diagnosis accuracy, crafting efficiency, prevention — depends on having something to work with. Build the foundation before chasing specialization.

**Can the physician role defend itself?**

Generally it is a support role, so expect to rely on others in a direct confrontation. That said, a physician who understands poisons, conditions, and body mechanics often has indirect options that other roles do not.

**How do I know if my physician build is working?**

Watch your emergency count. If crises become less frequent and retainers recover faster, your build is doing its job. If you are constantly reacting and never preparing, it is time to shift points toward prevention and supply.
