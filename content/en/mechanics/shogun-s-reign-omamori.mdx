Set in Edo-period Japan, [Shogun's Reign](https://www.roblox.com/games/106568491289620/Shoguns-Reign) asks a simple question with a brutal answer: can you start with almost nothing and end up ruling everything? The experience, published by the creator group Shogun's Reign, advertises farming, labour, towns, villages, professions, reputation and a climb through the social hierarchy all the way to Shogun. Crucially, only one player can hold that top position at a time, so the ladder is a race as much as a grind.

This guide stays close to what the official Roblox listing actually says. Where players have filled in gaps, that is labelled as community practice. Where nothing is documented, that is stated plainly rather than guessed at.

## What the official page actually advertises

The official Roblox record (place 106568491289620) was created on 25 July 2026 and last updated on 13 October 2026, with roughly 7.3 million visits and around 3,300 players online at the time of retrieval. That level of traffic on a young experience tells you the loop is engaging enough to retain a crowd, but it also means competition for scarce roles is real.

- Starting with almost nothing and earning your first Ryō through farming, labour and trades
- Towns, villages, shrines, estates and roads to explore
- Professions to master

- Ranks climbing from servant upward to warrior, noble or feudal lord

Everything beyond that list is either community observation or undocumented. ## Why the keyword matters: reputation is the real currency

Most players fixate on Ryō, because Ryō is what you earn first and what gates your early options. But the advertised progression runs on two tracks: wealth and reputation. Farming and labour build the first; visible, useful activity in a town or village builds the second.

The practical implication is that hoarding Ryō without participating in public life stalls your climb. Ranks up to Shogun are described as part of the same system as reputation, which suggests social standing and material wealth are meant to reinforce each other. The exact thresholds are not documented, so the honest advice is: watch the in-game UI for reputation or rank indicators and note what changed them. That is verification, not guesswork.

## Choosing a lane without a published profession list

Because the official page does not list professions, no guide can honestly tell you "pick X class." What you can do is reason from the three advertised earning routes.

- Farming suits patience and repetition. It is likely the lowest-barrier entry, which matches "earning your first Ryō."
- Labour suits players who want to move around, since it pairs naturally with the advertised roads, towns and estates.
- Trades suit players who want to deal with other people, which is where reputation compounds fastest.

A sensible first session is to sample each route for a short period, note which one produces Ryō reliably for you, and then go deep. Depth beats breadth, because "master a profession" implies a ceiling that broad dabbling will not reach.

## Roleplay and social climbing: separate the two

This is where players most often confuse mechanics with etiquette. Roleplaying a servant, a merchant or a ronin is player practice, not a documented system. The game gives you roles and a hierarchy; how you perform within them is community convention.

Practical habits that experienced roleplayers tend to follow:

- Stay in character when interacting with strangers in towns and on roads, and break character only in out-of-character channels.
- Ask before assuming authority over someone. Claiming a title you have not earned is the fastest way to get ignored.
- Do small visible jobs. Reputation, as far as anyone can tell without documentation, responds to being seen contributing.
- Do not bargain for a position by quoting a number you read on a third-party site. Verify in game first.

None of this guarantees advancement. The single Shogun slot means the system is zero-sum at the top, and other players are working the same ladder.
