## The Progression Loop: Ryō Before Rank

The most important structural fact about Shogun's Reign is the order of operations. Ryō comes first, and it comes from work rather than combat or exploration. The official description specifically names farming, labour and trades as the entry points. That tells you the early game is economic, not social.

Practically, this means new players should treat their first session as a job search rather than an adventure. Walk the roads, reach a town, and look for whatever work is being offered in the world itself rather than asking in chat and hoping. Because the game does not document pay rates, the only reliable way to compare two jobs is to do each one and watch your own balance. That is not a satisfying answer, but it is an honest one, and it beats repeating a number someone invented on a forum.

Reputation is the second currency, and the official listing ties it directly to rank. It does not say whether reputation accrues passively, from specific tasks, or from social interactions. If you want to know what moves your reputation bar, the in-game interface is the only place to check — look for a stats panel, an inventory screen, or a profile readout, and watch which numbers change after each activity.

## Professions: What We Know and What We Don't

The word "profession" in the official description is doing a lot of work with very little definition. We know professions exist and that mastering one is part of the intended arc. We do not know the list, the tiers within each profession, or whether switching is allowed once you commit.

Third-party guides have been published on various fan sites claiming to enumerate professions and rank ladders, but these are community observations rather than developer documentation, and they should be treated as unverified until you confirm them in-game. A safe approach is to pick the profession that matches the economic activity you already enjoy, since farming, labour and trades are the three categories the developers themselves named. If you like the loop you are already doing, you are less likely to burn out before your reputation starts mattering.

If you genuinely need the list before committing, the cleanest verification method is to check the in-game profession selection screen directly. Whatever appears there is authoritative; whatever appears in a fan guide is a claim about a build that may have changed in the most recent update.

## Roleplay Etiquette That Players Actually Follow

Because the social hierarchy is central, roleplay behavior carries real social weight even though the official page does not codify it. The following is player practice rather than documented mechanics, but it is widely observed:

- Bow before addressing someone of visibly higher rank. Bowing is free and it signals that you understand the setting.
- Do not claim a title you have not earned. Announcing yourself as a lord when your reputation is at servant level is the fastest way to get ignored or mocked.
- Stay in character in public areas, and use out-of-character channels or whispers for mechanical questions.
- Treat the Shogun position as contested. Whoever holds it earned it through the systems the game documents, and challenging them is a roleplay decision with roleplay consequences.

None of this is enforced by code as far as the official listing indicates. It is enforced by the players around you.

## Finding Roles and Building Reputation Over Time

The fastest route into faction play is usually to become useful to someone already established. Offer labour before asking for anything. Haul goods, work a plot, or take a trade task for a wealthier player, and you build a relationship that can later translate into a position.

The Shogun's Reign page does not document a formal faction system, so it is unclear whether joining a house or clan is a coded mechanic or purely a matter of player agreement. Verify this in-game before promising anything to anyone. If a coded system exists, it will appear in your menus; if it does not, the arrangement is entirely social and depends on the people involved showing up.

The one hard constraint to remember is the single Shogun slot. Whatever the path to it looks like, only one player can hold it, which means the top of the ladder is a competition rather than a milestone you simply reach.
