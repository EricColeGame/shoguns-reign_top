## Roleplay, Etiquette and Social Climbing

The description's emphasis on ranks up to Shogun makes this as much a social game as an economic one. The following is **player practice**, not documented mechanics — conventions the community tends to expect rather than rules the game enforces:

- **Address higher ranks formally.** In an Edo-period setting, modes of address carry meaning. Using them signals you understand the setting.
- **Stay in character in public spaces.** Shrines, estates and town centres read as roleplay zones; roads and fields are often where people relax.
- **Don't beg for Ryō.** Asking for handouts is a fast way to burn reputation with the players who control opportunities.
- **Find a role, not just a rank.** Lords and nobles need servants, guards, cooks, messengers and clerks. Arriving with a function is more useful than arriving with ambition.
- **Be patient with the top.** One Shogun means a lot of people competing for one seat. Most players will never hold it, and that is by design.

None of this guarantees advancement. It simply makes you easier to trust, and trust is the only currency that isn't printed on the official page.
