Every campaign in **Shogun's Reign** begins with the same question: which clan will you lead? Pick poorly and you will spend your opening hours defending a starving province with a handful of levy spearmen; pick well and your Shogun's Reign clan snowballs into a war machine before your rivals finish their first treaty. This guide walks through how the Shogun's Reign clan system actually works, from faction traits and unit counters to the diplomacy timing that decides who wins the late game.

## Why Your Shogun's Reign Clan Choice Decides the Campaign

Clan selection is not a cosmetic choice. In most shogun-era strategy games, your clan sets your starting province, your economy, your unit roster, and the diplomatic attitude every neighbor holds toward you on turn one. Those advantages compound. A clan that earns extra income early can afford a second army a few turns sooner, which lets it take a province sooner, which funds a third army.

The reverse is also true. A clan that starts with a weak economy and hostile neighbors spends its first stretch of the campaign reacting instead of acting, and reacting rarely wins a domination victory.

The practical takeaway: decide what kind of campaign you want before you look at the clan roster. Your Shogun's Reign clan should match your temperament, because you will be stuck with it for dozens of hours.

| Clan Archetype | Core Strength | Main Weakness | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggressive / Expansionist | Cheaper early infantry, faster recruitment | Fragile economy, many enemies | Players who want constant offense |
| Defensive / Fortified | Stronger garrisons, cheaper repairs | Slow expansion, passive allies | Patient turtle-then-strike players |
| Economic / Mercantile | Trade income, cheaper buildings | Weak standing army | Builders who plan far ahead |
| Naval / Coastal | Better ships, raiding income | Vulnerable to landings | Map-control players |
| Diplomatic / Scheming | Cheaper treaties, better relations | Depends on AI goodwill | Players who win without big battles |

## Core Clan Systems and Combat Roles

### How Clan Identity Is Built

Four systems usually define a clan: a starting trait, a technology path, a roster of unique or elite units, and a set of retainers or generals. Traits are permanent and shape your whole campaign. Technology is a choice you make every few turns. Rosters and retainers are the payoff you unlock later.

Community reports consistently suggest that traits matter most in the first third of a campaign, while unique units matter most in the final third. Plan accordingly — do not pick a clan for a late-game unit if you cannot survive the early game with its trait.

### Unit Roles and the Counter Triangle

Feudal combat in Shogun's Reign leans on a rock-paper-scissors relationship between spears, bows, and cavalry. Ignoring it is the fastest way to lose a battle you should have won on numbers alone.

| Unit Type | Strong Against | Weak Against | Best Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spear infantry | Cavalry | Massed archers | Holding a line, screening flanks |
| Bow / ranged | Spear infantry | Cavalry charges | Softening a line before melee |
| Cavalry | Archers, skirmishers | Spear walls | Flanking, chasing routers |
| Siege engines | Walls and gates | Open-field fights | Taking fortresses, breaking stalemates |
| Elite / retainer units | Generalists | Sustained attrition | Winning a decisive engagement |

Two more rules worth internalizing: never charge cavalry into a spear formation from the front, and never let archers stand unsupported. A single unit of cavalry in your backline can erase an entire ranged wing in seconds.

## How to Pick a Starting Clan in Shogun's Reign

Instead of asking "which clan is strongest," ask "which clan fits the campaign I want to play." That question has a real answer, and it changes based on your experience level.

| Your Goal | What to Look For | What to Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Fast first conquest | Aggressive trait, cheap early infantry | Slow-start economic clans |
| Learning campaign | Defensive trait, protected borders | Coastal starts with many landing zones |
| Economic victory | Port access, trade income bonuses | Inland clans with no coastline |
| Domination victory | Central map position, balanced roster | Periphery clans far from the center |
| Naval map control | Ship bonuses, long coastline | Landlocked starting provinces |

If the Shogun's Reign clan roster draws on history — as most games set in this period do — you will recognize names like Oda, Takeda, Uesugi, Tokugawa, Shimazu, and Date. Historically, Oda is associated with rapid central expansion, Takeda with cavalry, Uesugi with disciplined warrior elites, and Shimazu with a peripheral power base that punches above its weight. Treat those as thematic starting points, not as guarantees about how any specific game balances them.

## Early-Game Clan Strategy: A Step-by-Step Plan

Most campaigns fail in the opening, not the endgame. The Shogun's Reign clan that consolidates before it expands almost always outlasts the one that sprints into a two-front war.

| Phase | Objective | Do Not |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidation | Secure your home province, build income buildings | Start a second war |
| First Conquest | Take one adjacent province with a clean border | Overextend past your supply |
| Stabilization | Rebuild the army, sign non-aggression pacts | Break a treaty for a quick grab |
| Directional Expansion | Push one direction only | Fight everyone simultaneously |
| Endgame Push | Break alliances on your own terms | Let a rival snowball unchecked |

### The Opening Checklist

- **Turn one through five:** queue economic buildings before military ones. Armies do not pay for themselves.
- **Scout everything.** Knowing whether your neighbor is fielding spears or cavalry decides what you recruit.
- **Fight only battles you chose.** A defensive battle on your own soil with reinforcements nearby is worth three risky field fights.
- **Keep one reserve stack.** An uncommitted army deters invasions without firing a shot.
- **Watch the alliance web.** If two neighbors sign a pact, your window to attack one of them just closed.

### Combat Fundamentals That Decide Battles

Positioning beats unit count in almost every engagement. Anchor your line on terrain, put archers on elevation, and hold cavalry in reserve until the enemy commits. When you do commit, hit the flank or the rear — a rear charge triggers a morale collapse far faster than a frontal grind.

Morale, not casualties, ends most battles. Breaking three units in quick succession often routs the entire enemy army, so concentrate your damage rather than spreading it across the whole line.

## Diplomacy, Alliances, and Common Mistakes

Diplomacy in Shogun's Reign is a resource like any other. Every treaty you sign buys you time, and time is what an underdog clan needs most.

| Diplomatic State | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Neutral neighbor, shared border | Sign a non-aggression pact before you expand elsewhere |
| Friendly but stronger than you | Trade and gift lightly; avoid becoming a vassal in all but name |
| Ally of your target | Isolate them first, or expect a two-front war |
| Weakened rival | Pressure immediately — do not let them rebuild |
| Betrayed you once | Treat as permanently hostile; treaties with them are temporary |

### Mistakes That Sink a Shogun's Reign Clan

- **Fighting on two fronts.** Split attention is split strength. Finish one war before starting another.
- **Neglecting farms and markets.** A large army with a weak economy disbands itself.
- **Auto-resolving every battle.** Manual control wins engagements that auto-resolve loses.
- **Ignoring naval power.** Coastal clans that skip ships get raided all campaign long.
- **Hoarding gold.** Treasury sitting idle is production you never built.
- **Breaking treaties too early.** The reputation penalty lingers far longer than the province you gained.

## FAQ

**What is the best starting clan in Shogun's Reign?**
There is no single best pick, because the answer depends on your playstyle. If you want a forgiving first campaign, choose a clan with a defensive trait and protected borders. If you already understand the combat system, an aggressive clan with cheap early infantry will snowball fastest. Player experience suggests matching the clan trait to your temperament matters more than any stat bonus.

**Can you switch clans mid-campaign?**
In most shogun-era strategy games, your clan choice is locked for the entire campaign. That is exactly why the decision deserves a few minutes of thought before you click start — you are committing to a starting position, an economy, and a diplomatic reputation all at once.

**How do I stop a rival clan from snowballing?**
Act early. Sign a pact with their neighbors, cut their trade routes, and take one province from them before their army outnumbers yours. Community reports are consistent on this point: a rival left alone for a long stretch of the campaign becomes nearly impossible to dislodge later.

**Is the Shogun's Reign clan system friendly to new players?**
Yes, if you start on a lower difficulty and pick a clan with a strong defensive position. The learning curve is mostly about economy management and unit counters, both of which you can practice safely while your borders stay quiet. Once you can win a war without losing your income, move up in difficulty and try an aggressive Shogun's Reign clan for a completely different campaign.
