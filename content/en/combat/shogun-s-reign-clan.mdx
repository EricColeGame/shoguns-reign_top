## What the official page actually advertises

The verified official description is short and specific. Shogun's Reign advertises:

- Farming and labour as a way to earn Ryō
- Towns and villages to explore
- Professions to pursue
- Reputation as a progression currency of sorts
- Ranks that extend all the way up to Shogun
- Competition for authority, including that top position

That is the whole advertised feature set. Notably, the official page does **not** document a code system, a list of professions, rank requirements, pay rates, or a housing system. That matters, because a lot of search traffic around this game assumes those systems exist in a form the developer never confirmed. If you see a guide promising a working promo code, treat it as unverified — the official page documents none.

## Starting out: Ryō before reputation

The advertised loop starts with money, not status. According to the official description, farming and labour are the documented ways to earn your first Ryō, so the early game is deliberately unglamorous. You are not handed a sword and a title; you are handed work.

Practically, that means your first sessions should be about learning the map and finding repeatable work rather than chasing a rank. Explore the towns and villages the page mentions, note where players congregate, and watch how experienced players move between locations. Because the game is a roleplaying experience as well as a progression one, the way you behave while doing that labour matters to the people around you — more on that below.

What the sources do not tell us is how much any job pays, how long a shift takes, or whether some work is gated behind reputation. Those specifics are not in the official description, and inventing them would be guesswork. The honest way to find out is to play the loop yourself or ask in-game.

## Ranks, reputation and the climb to Shogun

The headline promise is the ladder: servant to warrior, noble, or feudal lord, with a single Shogun at the top. The official description confirms that ranks up to Shogun exist and that authority is competitive. It does not confirm how many ranks there are, what each one requires, or how the Shogun is selected or replaced.

That gap is where most third-party guides get speculative. Community guides — secondary observations by players, not developer documentation — commonly describe reputation as the driver of advancement and treat the Shogun position as contested rather than permanent. That is consistent with the official wording about competition for authority, but it is still player interpretation. If a guide gives you an exact reputation number for a specific rank, verify it in-game before trusting it, because the developer has not published one.

A sensible approach: treat reputation as a long-term investment, not a checklist. Do the work the game advertises, stay visible in populated areas, and let your standing accumulate rather than hunting for shortcuts that may not exist.

## Professions: what we know and what we don't

Professions are advertised. A profession *list* is not. That distinction is the single most common source of bad information about Shogun's Reign. Guides that hand you a tidy menu of trades are either paraphrasing what they saw in-game or filling the gap themselves.

If you want to know which professions are available right now, the reliable method is direct observation:

1. Read the in-game interface and any signage or NPC dialogue in towns.
2. Ask other players what they do for income — this is normal practice in roleplay-heavy servers.
3. Check the official page again after updates. The place was last updated on October 13, 2026, and progression games change frequently.

Until something is written down by the developer, treat every profession list as a snapshot, not a spec.

## Roleplay etiquette and finding your place

Because Shogun's Reign is a roleplaying experience, mechanical progress and social progress are tangled together. The following is player practice, not documented mechanics, but it is widely useful:

- **Stay in character in public areas.** Towns and roads are shared spaces; breaking character mid-scene disrupts other players' stories.
- **Ask before you intrude.** Estates, shrines and households often have owners or players running them. A short out-of-character question saves a lot of friction.
- **Respect the hierarchy you're trying to climb.** If you want to reach warrior or noble status, behaving like one before you have the rank tends to backfire socially.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** The game starts everyone near the bottom, which is precisely why reputation earned through courtesy compounds.

None of this is enforced by the official description. It is simply how these communities tend to work, and it is worth more than any shortcut.
