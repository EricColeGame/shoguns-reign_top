## Your first hour: earning Ryō without guessing

The advertised starting loop is farming and labour. That means your early minutes are not about choosing a build or a class — they are about finding work and doing it. Because the official page does not publish pay rates, avoid trusting any specific number you read online. Instead, treat your first session as an information-gathering exercise:

1. Look for the jobs or labour areas near where you spawn, and note what each one asks of you.
2. Do a short run of each type of work and watch your Ryō balance change.
3. Compare the time each task takes against what it pays, in your own play session.
4. Bank a small buffer before you start wandering, so an unexpected cost does not strand you.

This is not glamorous, but it matches how the game is advertised: you start with almost nothing and build from there. Players commonly report that early progress feels slow by design, but that is player practice, not documented mechanics — your own session is the only reliable benchmark for how fast you earn.

## Money versus reputation

The description separates wealth and reputation, which is a meaningful distinction. Ryō is the economic track: it buys you things and reflects your labour. Reputation is the social track: it reflects how you are regarded, and it is the thing that plausibly gates higher ranks. The official page does not explain how reputation is earned or how much of it any rank requires, so do not plan around a formula you found on a wiki.

A practical approach is to ask, in-game, what the rank above you actually requires. Other players are often willing to explain the visible rank requirements in their own menus. If someone quotes an exact threshold, ask them to show you where the game displays it. That is a far better filter than trusting a screenshot from months ago, especially given the place was updated on 2026-10-13.

## Professions and climbing the hierarchy

Professions are advertised but not enumerated, so the honest advice is: choose based on what you enjoy doing repeatedly, not on a tier list that does not officially exist. If you like the farming loop, lean into production. If you prefer moving through the map, look at what services towns and villages actually need. Specialising tends to be more efficient than dabbling, but the game does not publish a respec cost or a profession cap, so if you want to switch, the only way to know the consequences is to check your own menus before committing.

The hierarchy is the long game. Ranks run up to Shogun, and the Shogun position is explicitly competitive — a single slot that players contend for rather than a rank everyone eventually reaches. That has two implications. First, most players will spend their time in intermediate roles, which is fine; the roleplay and progression is the point. Second, because only one person can hold the top spot, any strategy that depends on becoming Shogun is a strategy with a very low ceiling. Build a character and a reputation you would enjoy even if you never reach the summit.

## Roleplay etiquette and finding a role

Roleplay advice below is player practice, not documented mechanics. Servers in this genre tend to reward restraint over improvisation.

- Address people by their apparent rank until told otherwise. Deference costs nothing and signals you understand the setting.
- Do not interrupt a scene in progress. Watch for a pause, then approach.
- Give your character a job and a reason to be where they are. "I am delivering rice to the estate" opens more doors than standing silently.
- Keep out-of-character chatter in whatever channel the server uses for it, if one exists.
- If you want a specific role, ask in a calm moment rather than mid-scene.
