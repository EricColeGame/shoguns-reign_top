## Earning your first Ryō without guessing

The one concrete economic detail the description supports is the starting point: almost nothing. Your first Ryō comes from farming, labour or trades. Beyond that, no official pay rate exists to quote, and inventing one would be irresponsible — rates in games like this are tuned constantly, and a number written today could be wrong by the weekend.

The practical approach is to treat your first session as reconnaissance. Walk the roads, visit a town and a village, look at what interactive stations exist, and read the on-screen prompts when you approach them. Because the game is a roleplay experience, the more useful question is often not "which task pays most" but "which task lets me stand near other players." Reputation in roleplay-driven games usually accrues through visibility and consistency, not just currency.

If you want hard numbers, verify them yourself: perform a task, note the payout, repeat it, and compare across two or three activities. That is more reliable than any third-party table, including ones published this week.

## Player practice: how people actually roleplay here

Everything in this section is community practice, not documented rules. Treat it as etiquette that tends to work in Edo-period roleplay servers generally.

Approach scenes rather than demanding them. Walk, don't sprint through a shrine or an estate. Bow when you enter a lord's presence, even if no one prompts you. Speak in character when you're near other players who are in character, and use out-of-character channels for logistics.

Finding a role is usually a matter of proximity. Stand near a workplace, respond to what's happening, and let someone give you a task. New players who announce their ambition to become Shogun in the first hour tend to get ignored; players who reliably show up and do small jobs get pulled into larger scenes. None of this is guaranteed to produce a rank, and none of it is written into the game's rules.
